Question: Check Whether a Number is a Prime Number

Explanation:

A Prime Number is a natural number greater than 1 that has exactly two factors: 1 and itself.

A prime number is divisible only by 1 and the number itself without leaving a remainder.

Examples of prime numbers are 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, 17 and 19.

Logic:

1. Read a number.
2. Check whether the number is greater than 1.
3. Initialize a counter to count the factors.
4. Check divisibility from 1 up to the given number.
5. If the number is divisible without a remainder, increment the counter.
6. If the counter is exactly 2, it is a Prime Number.
7. Otherwise, it is not a Prime Number.

Example:

Input: 7

Factors of 7: 1 and 7

Since 7 has exactly two factors,

Output: Prime Number

Test Cases:

1. Input: 7
   Output: Prime Number

2. Input: 13
   Output: Prime Number

3. Input: 9
   Output: Not a Prime Number
   Reason: Factors are 1, 3 and 9.

4. Input: 1
   Output: Not a Prime Number
   Reason: 1 has only one factor.

5. Input: 2
   Output: Prime Number
   Reason: 2 has exactly two factors, 1 and 2.
